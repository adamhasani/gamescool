Night Brawler brings four street corners to Gamescool

Meet Dex and Biscuit in Night Brawler, a free browser brawler. Get the controls and learn how dodges, tackles and air combos work.

30 Sep 2026 · 2 min read · Gamescool

Night Brawler is out on Gamescool, free to play in your browser with no sign-in required. This single-player pixel-art street brawler follows Dex and his dog, Biscuit, as they take back four street corners in one night. You punch, kick and dodge through a neon district, with story scenes between the fights.

From the alley to the roof

A gang is stripping the district's neon signs for Mr. Blackout. You follow them from the alley behind a shuttered arcade through an all-night diner and a subway station, before facing the boss on the roof.

Each corner has its own chapter, with scenes before and after the fight. Between chapters, a city map shows cleared stops lighting back up as Biscuit leads Dex toward the next one.

Keyboard, controller and phone controls

On keyboard, move with WASD or the arrow keys, press J to punch and K to kick. Use Space to jump, L or Shift to dodge, and I or E to send Biscuit in. Press Esc to pause; left-click and right-click also punch and kick.

On a controller, move with the left stick or d-pad. Press X to punch, Y to kick, A to jump and B to dodge. Either LB or RB sends the dog, and Start pauses.

On phones, move with the stick under your left thumb and use the Punch, Kick, Jump, Dodge and Dog buttons on the right. Upright play works too, but holding your phone sideways gives the best view.

Try a perfect dodge and an air combo

Dodge just as an enemy hit lands to slow time and add a charge to Biscuit's tackle. Press Punch or Kick promptly afterward to turn your next attack into a flying knee that breaks guards. With a charge available, the Dog button sends Biscuit to tackle an enemy and knock them down.

For an air combo, punch once to three times, then kick to launch an enemy. Jump immediately to chase them, punch in the air to keep them up, then kick to slam them down. Those linked moves and the dodge-to-tackle rhythm give you something concrete to try on your first run through the alley.
